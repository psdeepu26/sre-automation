+# Decision Journals: Debug Your Judgment, Not Just the Outcome
+
+**Personal Growth · Decision Making · 17 July 2026**
+
+## 🧒 Like you're 5
+
+Imagine choosing an umbrella because the sky is dark. Later, the sun comes out. That does not mean taking the umbrella was foolish—you made a sensible choice using what you knew.
+
+A decision journal is a tiny note you write **before** the weather changes. It helps Future You remember what Present You saw, expected, and worried about.
+
+## ↓ Now let’s call it what it really is ↓
+
+## 🔧 For the engineer
+
+Humans routinely confuse **decision quality** with **outcome quality**. This is outcome bias: rewarding a weak process after a lucky result, or rejecting a strong process after bad luck. A decision journal preserves the inputs and model state that existed at decision time.
+
+### Four fields that matter
+
+- **Context:** State the decision, constraints, deadline, reversibility, and cost of delay.
+- **Beliefs:** List assumptions and evidence. Separate observed facts from interpretations.
+- **Forecast:** Write expected outcomes with probabilities—not vague labels like “likely.”
+- **Review trigger:** Choose when or under what signal the decision will be reviewed.
+
+```text
+Decision: Protect two 90-minute focus blocks each week
+Why now: Important learning keeps losing to reactive work
+Assumptions:
+  - 70%: calendar protection survives normal interruptions
+  - 60%: two blocks produce one useful artifact per week
+Failure signal: <3 completed blocks over 3 weeks
+Review date: 2026-08-07
+Next action: reserve Tuesday and Thursday blocks
+```
+
+**Calibration beats certainty.** If events you label “70% likely” happen roughly seven times out of ten, your judgment is calibrated. Track enough decisions and your recurring blind spots become measurable.
+
+### Review without rewriting history
+
+1. **Score process first:** Were assumptions explicit? Were alternatives considered? Was evidence current?
+2. **Score outcome second:** What happened, and how much came from skill versus luck?
+3. **Extract one model update:** Change one assumption, threshold, or check—not your entire worldview.
+
+Use journals for consequential or repeated decisions. Logging every tiny choice creates toil; logging none creates untestable intuition.
+
+## ⚡ Micro-action (5 minutes)
+
+Pick one decision you are currently postponing. Write five lines: **decision, current evidence, biggest assumption, probability of success, review date**. Make the choice only after those lines are visible.
