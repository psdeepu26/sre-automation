Sequence Risk: Same Average, Different Outcome

+

Financial HealthRetirement Planning15 Aug 2026

+ +
+
🧒 Like you're 5
+

Two families pack identical lunch boxes for a week. One eats the biggest meals first; the other saves them for later. If nobody removes food, both finish with the same total.

+

Now imagine each family must give away one meal every day. The family hit by small meals first can run out, even though both boxes had the same average meal size. Investments behave similarly when you withdraw money: bad returns early can do lasting damage.

+
+ +
↓ Same returns. Different order. Withdrawals make order matter. ↓
+ +
+

🔧 For the engineer

+
+
Sequence-of-returns riskRisk that return order, not long-run average alone, changes portfolio survival while cash flows enter or leave.
+
Why accumulation differsWith no withdrawals, multiplication is commutative: ₹100 × 1.2 × 0.8 equals ₹100 × 0.8 × 1.2.
+
Why decumulation breaks symmetryWithdrawals remove units after losses. Fewer units remain to participate in recovery, creating path dependence.
+
Danger zoneFinal working years plus early retirement. Portfolio is large, contributions stop, and withdrawals begin.
+
Useful defensesCash/bond runway, flexible spending, diversified allocation, periodic rebalancing, and avoiding forced sales after sharp declines.
+
Bad shortcutA high historical average return does not guarantee a safe fixed withdrawal. Model return paths, inflation, fees, taxes, and spending flexibility.
+
Toy examplePortfolio: ₹100. Withdraw ₹10 yearly. Returns: +20%, −20%.

Good-first: 100×1.2−10=110; 110×0.8−10=78.
Bad-first: 100×0.8−10=70; 70×1.2−10=74.
+
SRE analogyAverage capacity can look healthy while failure timing causes an outage. Retirement plan needs headroom and graceful degradation, not average-only sizing.
+
+

Educational example only—not personalized investment advice.

+

Open your retirement sheet. Add one stress row: assume a 25% market drop in year one, then apply your planned annual withdrawal. Note whether you could fund 12–24 months without selling growth assets.
