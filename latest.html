💰 Sinking Funds: Make Predictable Surprises Boring

+

Financial Health25 July 2026Cash-flow planning

+ +
+
🧒 Like you're 5
+

Your school trip costs ₹12,000 next year. Waiting until trip day makes it feel like a giant surprise. Putting ₹1,000 into a jar every month turns it into twelve tiny, easy payments.

+

A sinking fund is that jar. It is for expenses that feel unexpected only because they do not happen every month: insurance, vehicle service, festivals, travel, repairs, or replacing a phone.

+
+ +
↓ Now call it what it really is: pre-funding a known future liability ↓
+ +
+

🔧 For the engineer

+
monthly contribution = (target cost − current balance) ÷ months remaining
+
+
Sinking fund vs emergency fundSinking fund handles known or estimable costs. Emergency fund handles genuinely uncertain shocks such as job loss or urgent medical needs.
+
Separate ledgerUse one bank sub-account or one spreadsheet ledger with named buckets. Separation prevents spending money twice in your mental model.
+
Estimate with headroomUse last cost plus a 5–10% buffer for inflation or scope drift. Review estimate when renewal quotes arrive.
+
Match risk to horizonMoney needed within 1–2 years should prioritize liquidity and capital safety, not volatile equity returns.
+
Automate after paydaySchedule transfers immediately after income lands. Treat contributions like recurring bills, not whatever remains at month-end.
+
Reconcile quarterlyUpdate target, due date, balance, and contribution. When one goal completes, redirect freed cash instead of letting lifestyle spend absorb it.
+
+

Example: Annual insurance ₹36,000 due in 9 months, with ₹9,000 saved: (₹36,000 − ₹9,000) ÷ 9 = ₹3,000/month. Add a 10% buffer only if renewal cost is uncertain.

+
+ +
+
⚡ Micro-action (5 minutes)
+

Write your next three non-monthly expenses with amount and due month. Calculate each monthly contribution using the formula. Automate the smallest one today.
